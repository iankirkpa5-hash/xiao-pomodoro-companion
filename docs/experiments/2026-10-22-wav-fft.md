## FFT And Window Conventions

Each FFT subtracts the mean of its own input segment and applies a periodic
Hann window, `w[n] = 0.5 - 0.5*cos(2*pi*n/N)`. No high-pass filter, preview gain,
resampling, gap filling or zero padding is applied.

One-sided peak amplitude uses `abs(rfft(x*w)) / sum(w)` and doubles interior
bins only. PSD uses `abs(rfft(x*w))**2 / (fs*sum(w*w))` with the same one-sided
endpoint rule. Amplitude and PSD have different normalization: the integrated
PSD equals `sum((x*w)**2)/sum(w*w)`, not generally the unwindowed RMS squared.
Amplitude normalization is exact for an isolated bin-centered sinusoid away
from DC/Nyquist; speech and off-bin tones spread across bins.

Definitions follow [NumPy FFT conventions](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/routines.fft.html)
and the [Hann window definition](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.hanning.html)
(periodic here, equivalent to `np.hanning(N+1)[:-1]`).

| Window | Samples | Bin spacing fs/N | Hann main lobe, null-to-null |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 40 ms | 640 | 25 Hz | approximately 100 Hz |
| 250 ms | 4000 | 4 Hz | approximately 16 Hz |
| Full 5 s | 80000 | 0.2 Hz | approximately 0.8 Hz |

Bin spacing is not a guarantee of resolving two nearby sounds. The synthetic
440 + 470 Hz example has broadened, displaced maxima in the 40 ms result, but
clearly separated lobes in the 250 ms result. A longer window helps separate
nearby steady tones while averaging over more temporal change. Whole-record
speech is not stationary, so the finest bin grid is not automatically the
best speech description. The synthetic example is not microphone evidence.

Real excerpts share center 0.535 s: 40 ms starts at 0.515 s and 250 ms at
0.410 s. The center comes from the highest-RMS 10 ms block after whole-record
DC removal, not a detector for voiced speech. The waveform detail retains the
whole-record mean convention; each excerpt FFT removes its own mean.
